prices, upper end), labour and services from typical local rates. They are not quotes.

Pilot design (from `docs/limitations.md`, section 4): stage 1 on a closed course, then stage 2 on one
real corridor (a campus gate or school crossing plus a bus stop) with up to 50 equipped vehicles,
10 stop/crossing beacons and 20 blind or low-vision participants over about three months.

**Minimal version (≈ $3,200, estimate):** stage 1 only, on a closed course: 10 vehicle beacons ($240),
10 wristbands ($190), 2 loaner phones ($300), kit ($150), 10 participants × 1 session ($300),
1 workshop ($300), spotters 5 days ($300), ethics/insurance ($1,000), 15 % contingency (≈ $420); subtotal $2,780.

What drives cost: people (compensation, guides, workshops) more than hardware. A rider-phone pilot
with an operator's app replaces most vehicle beacons at zero hardware cost.

What to measure is listed in `docs/limitations.md` (section 4):
detection range, warning lead time, false alarms per hour, battery life, usability.
